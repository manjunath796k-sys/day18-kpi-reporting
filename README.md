# Day 18 – KPI Reporting

## Project Overview

This project focuses on **KPI Reporting and Business Metrics** using the Capstone Customers and Orders dataset.

The objective is to convert cleaned data into meaningful business KPIs that can support decision-making.

The analysis was performed using **Python and Pandas**.

---

## Learning Objectives

* Understand the difference between KPIs and vanity metrics
* Calculate important business KPIs
* Understand customer retention and repeat customers
* Perform revenue analysis by region and category
* Perform cohort analysis using customer JoinDate
* Identify the best and lowest-performing KPI/category
* Understand how KPIs can be misleading
* Distinguish between meaningful business changes and noise

---

## Dataset

The analysis uses the Capstone dataset containing:

* `Capstone Customers.csv`
* `Capstone Orders.csv`

## Cohort Analysis

Customers were grouped according to their **JoinDate month**.

The cohort analysis tracks total sales generated by customers based on the month they joined.

This helps the business understand whether customers from different joining periods generate different levels of revenue.

## Best KPI

### Customer Retention Rate – 100.00%

Customer Retention Rate looks like the strongest KPI because all customers in the cleaned dataset have placed more than one order according to the assignment definition.

However, the 100% retention rate should be interpreted carefully. The business should validate the observation period and customer purchase history before concluding that actual long-term customer retention is 100%.

## Interpretation Questions

### 1. How can a KPI be reported in a misleading way?

Customer Retention Rate can be reported misleadingly by simply stating:

**"Customer retention is 100%."**

Although technically true according to the calculation used, this statement does not explain the measurement period or definition.

The honest version should explain that the 100% result comes from customers with more than one order divided by total customers in this dataset, and that the result should be validated against the actual business retention definition and time period.

---

### 2. Bad News vs Noise

A metric going down is bad news when the decline is consistent, meaningful, and supported by other business indicators.

It may be noise when the change is small or temporary.

To distinguish between them, I would compare trends across time, regions, categories, and customer cohorts rather than reacting to a single change.

---

## End-of-Day Questions

### What makes a metric a vanity metric?

A vanity metric looks impressive but does not provide meaningful information or support a specific business decision.

### Why does retention rate matter more than total customer count alone?

Total customer count shows how many customers exist, but retention rate shows whether existing customers continue to purchase and engage with the business.

### What would you ask before trusting a KPI for the first time?

I would ask how the KPI is defined, what data and time period were used, how it was calculated, and whether it is directly connected to a business decision.
